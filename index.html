1
Hook + real cause of the problem
0:00 — 2:30 · ~2.5 min
Hook them
Make them think: "This person understands my situation better than I do."
What to say
Opening pattern interrupt
"If you're [doing the obvious thing] and still [not getting the result] — it's not [what they think the problem is]." Challenge their existing assumption in the first 10 seconds.
Name the mechanism of the problem
"There is a specific [mechanism name] happening right now that is [consequence]. And until you understand it, nothing you do will fix it." Give the problem a name. Make it feel discovered, not invented.
Describe their current reality
Walk through exactly what their day/situation looks like. Specific details only. The more precisely you describe their reality, the more trust you build. They should be nodding.
Explain why their current solutions fail
"This is why [solution A] doesn't fix it. This is why [solution B] doesn't fix it." Validate their failed attempts — it wasn't their fault. They were solving the wrong problem.
Rules
First 30 seconds are everything. If they click away here, nothing else matters.
Never start with "Hi I'm [name]." Start with the hook — credentials come later.
Be specific. Vague = no trust. Specific = believable.
2
Solution mechanism + credibility
2:30 — 4:30 · ~2 min
Build belief
Make them think: "This is different from everything I've tried — and it makes sense."
What to say
Establish credibility fast
"My name is [name]. I [specific result you personally achieved / specific number of people you've helped / specific thing you do differently]." One to two sentences. Make it relevant to the problem — not a life story.
Introduce the solution mechanism
"The solution is what I call [Named Method]. Here's how it works: [3-step process explained simply]. This works when other approaches fail because it addresses [root cause from Section 1] directly."
Proof — 1 or 2 case studies
"[Client name / description] had [specific problem]. [One thing we changed]. [Specific measurable result] in [specific timeframe]." Keep it to 2-3 sentences each. Numbers and specifics only — no vague testimonials.
Rules
The mechanism must feel like the logical answer to the problem from Section 1. If the connection isn't obvious, it won't land.
Name the mechanism. A named thing feels real. An unnamed thing feels like opinion.
Case studies: specific person, specific problem, specific result, specific timeframe. All four.
3
Damaging admission + transition
4:30 — 5:30 · ~1 min
Build trust
Make them think: "This person is being honest with me. Everything else they've said is probably true."
What to say
Admit something genuinely negative
"Before I tell you about the programme — I need to be honest. This won't work if [real limitation]. It requires [real commitment or effort]. If [disqualifying condition] — don't join." Must be real. Never manufactured. Self-selects out bad-fit buyers and builds trust with good ones.
Transition to the product
"If [the right person description] — here's exactly what you get." One sentence bridge from the honest admission into the product presentation.
Rules
The admission must feel uncomfortable to say. If it's easy to say, it's not damaging enough.
It should eliminate people who would fail or refund — and make serious buyers more confident.
Never fake this. One fake admission destroys all the trust you've built.
4
The product
5:30 — 8:00 · ~2.5 min
Show the vehicle
Make them think: "This is exactly what I need. I can see myself going through this."
What to say
Name and position the product
"It's called [Product Name]. [One sentence connecting it directly to the solution mechanism from Section 2]." The product is the vehicle for implementing the mechanism — not a separate thing you're selling.
Walk through the deliverables
For each component: name it, explain what it does, and connect it to an outcome. "The [Component Name] — [what it is]. By the end of [timeframe] you'll have [specific outcome]." Outcomes over features. Always.
Paint the transformation
"[Timeframe] from now — [specific vivid description of their life after completing the programme]." Before versus after. Make it concrete and specific. The more clearly they can visualise themselves after, the more they want it.
Rules
Don't list every feature. Hit the major components and their outcomes. Move fast.
This section should feel like revelation, not salesmanship. You're showing the tool that implements what you've already proven works.
Every deliverable connects back to the mechanism. "This is where we [mechanism step]."
5
Offer + value stack + price
8:00 — 9:15 · ~1.25 min
Make it obvious
Make them think: "At this price, not buying this would be the wrong decision."
What to say
Stack the value
List every component with a realistic standalone value next to it. "[Component] — $[value]. [Component] — $[value]." Then add bonuses and their values. Then total everything. "Total value: $[X]." The anchor number should feel genuinely defensible — not inflated.
Reveal the price
"Your investment today — $[price]." State it clearly and confidently. No apology. No hedging. No "it's only" or "just." Pause after you say it. Let them process it against the anchor.
Explain why this price exists
"The reason it's priced here is [real reason — founding cohort, limited spots, proof-of-concept, etc.]." One honest sentence. Sets expectation that the price will go up and adds urgency without fake scarcity.
CTA
"Click the button below right now. [What happens next in one sentence]." Direct and specific. Tell them exactly what to do and what happens when they do it.
Rules
The value stack anchor makes the price feel like a discount — even if the price is premium. This is the whole point.
Never apologise for the price or justify it defensively. State it. Pause. Move on.
6
Close — two paths + urgency
9:15 — 10:00 · ~45 sec
Force the decision
Make them think: "I need to decide right now — and the right decision is obvious."
What to say
Path 1 — what happens if they do nothing
"Path one — you close this video and go back to [current approach]. [Timeframe] from now you're in roughly the same place. [Specific negative consequence]." Be honest, not dramatic. Realistic is more powerful than apocalyptic.
Path 2 — what happens if they join
"Path two — you click the button right now. [Timeframe] from now [specific vivid positive outcome]. [Next timeframe] — [next milestone]. [Final timeframe] — [transformation complete]." Mirror the same timeframes as Path 1 so the contrast is direct.
Real urgency + risk reversal
"[Real scarcity reason — cohort cap, closing date, founding price ending]. If you do the work and [it doesn't work] — [what you'll do]. You have nothing to lose." One sentence each. Then the final CTA.
Rules
The two-path close works because it makes the consequence of inaction vivid and real — not because it manipulates. Be honest about both paths.
Urgency must be real. Fake scarcity gets sensed immediately and destroys trust at the worst possible moment.
End with silence. Say the final CTA and stop talking. Don't fill the silence.
The one rule that runs through every section
You are not informing — you are shifting beliefs. Every sentence should move the viewer one step up the belief staircase: interesting → problem-aware → solutions don't work → there's a better way → this method makes sense → others succeeded → I can succeed → I trust this → I want this → I'll buy now. If a sentence doesn't move a belief, cut it.
